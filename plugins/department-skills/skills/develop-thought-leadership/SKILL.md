# Develop an evidence-backed point of view

Use when an expert has experience but needs an argument another practitioner could challenge. If the argument is already settled and only a social post is needed, preserve the argument and focus on channel and voice. Gather the expert's actual notes or interview, intended reader and decision, source locators, a real example, relevant contrary evidence and publication permissions.

## Build the argument

1. Write a one-sentence claim and the decision it would change for the reader. In an argument map, mark claims as observed, reported, inferred or proposed. Keep observed sample size, time and conditions with the claim. Two examples can demonstrate a possibility, not an industry-wide frequency.
2. Name the strongest plausible alternative explanation or case in which the advice fails. Use the best evidence for that case. When a competing account is hypothetical, label it as a test of the reasoning. Identify the observation that would distinguish competing explanations.
3. Narrow the claim to the setting that survives that test. Show the mechanism and the practical choice it changes. Retain the actual exception. Delete an unsupported number or rank; a projected effect needs a stated basis and cannot be presented as an observed outcome.
4. If the argument needs outside evidence, frame a focused question for research. Share only cleared context with an authorized research tool such as Claude Research. Ask for dated primary sources, pinpoint evidence, contradictory findings and gaps. Check returned sources directly before relying on the claims. Without access, keep the disputed section qualified or pending and draft the defensible remainder.
5. Draft in the expert's voice at the requested length or format. Quote only exact cleared words. Only use first person for the expert's own experience. End where the reader can make a different decision.

Deliver the thesis, strongest counterargument and its consequence, evidence-to-claim map, draft and one discriminating question or piece of evidence that would change the recommendation.
