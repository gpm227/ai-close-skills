# Define the brand

Plain AI writes positioning that could belong to any company in the category: "the trusted partner for innovative solutions." Take the name off and nobody could tell whose it is.

## You'll need

- **The offer.** What you deliver.
- **The alternatives,** including the customer doing it themselves or doing nothing.
- **Customer evidence.** Sales notes, call transcripts, reviews, a recent win and a recent loss. Give each a short ID.
- **What you can and can't deliver.**

If you have no customer conversations, the result is provisional. Say so and name the one question to ask a buyer next.

## Steps

1. Separate who pays, who uses it, and who benefits.
2. Find the moment a buyer starts looking, and the decision they're facing.
3. Compare the real alternatives on things buyers actually mentioned.
4. For every claimed difference, name what you do that makes it true and the evidence that you do it.
5. Pick one category a buyer would recognize.
6. Write a plain intro a customer could repeat, then the reason to choose you, then the limits of that promise.

## What you get back

A one-page brief: audience, buying moment, alternatives, category, promise, proof, open questions. Plus a one-sentence intro and a one-minute version for sales calls.

## The trap

One big customer chose you because you could start in a week. The obvious positioning becomes "the fastest to start." That's one win. Use it as an example until more buyers say the same thing.

## Check it

- Remove the company name. The description still says what you deliver.
- Every difference has a mechanism and evidence.
- A customer could repeat the one-sentence intro.
