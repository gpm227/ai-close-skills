# Define the brand

## Start with

Bring the offer, alternatives (including doing the work in-house), buyer interviews or sales notes, delivery constraints, and a recent win or loss. Give each source a short ID. When no customer interviews exist, build a provisional brief from first-party material and name the single most useful buyer question.

## Procedure

Separate purchaser, user and beneficiary. Extract the moment a buyer starts looking and the decision they face. Compare actual alternatives on dimensions the buyer mentioned. For each proposed distinction, point to a delivery mechanism the company controls and evidence it exists. Select one category a buyer would recognize. Write a plain introduction a customer could repeat, followed by the reason to choose the offer and the limits on that promise.

## Deliver

One-page positioning brief with audience, buying situation, alternatives, category, promise, proof and unresolved assumptions. Supply one short introduction and one longer account for a sales call.

## Check the result

Remove the brand name and check whether the explanation still identifies what the company delivers. A one-off customer win supplies an example; ask for broader evidence before making it the default reason to buy.
