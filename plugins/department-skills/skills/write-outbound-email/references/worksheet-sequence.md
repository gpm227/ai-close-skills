# Try the skill

Paste this request into your AI workspace with the skill instructions:

> Write a two-email sequence to an operations lead who compares weekly orders across restaurant locations. We offer a one-page worksheet with columns for store, date range and order count. It includes a row for recording store closures. The sender can email the worksheet upon request. We have no customer results to cite. Sound like one operator writing to another. The only next step is permission to send the worksheet.

## Example output

**1. Subject:** Weekly order comparisons

Comparing weekly orders across stores gets tricky when the dates cover different periods. We put together a one-page worksheet that keeps each store's date range beside its order count.

Would you like me to send it?

**2. Subject:** A place to flag closures

One detail I left out: the worksheet has a row for store closures. It gives you somewhere to flag a short week before you interpret a drop in orders.

Would the worksheet be useful?

## Check your draft

Both touches ask whether the buyer wants the worksheet. Try changing the close to “Would you like the worksheet, and are you free Thursday for a walkthrough?” A buyer who wants the worksheet still has a separate scheduling decision. For this brief, keep the worksheet offer.

Now try a brief where the buyer has asked to see the worksheet in a live walkthrough and the sender can offer Tuesday or Thursday. “Would Tuesday or Thursday work?” passes. The two options resolve one scheduling decision.

Try changing the request to say the sender can only share a blank worksheet. An offer to analyze the recipient's data would then fail the delivery check. A conversion or time-savings claim would also fail unless the brief supplied evidence.
