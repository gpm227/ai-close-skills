# The first call after a buyer’s request

All records are fictional. Use the facts here. Read the opening aloud and perform one branch as a rep.

## Input

- F1, yesterday's request from Casey, operations coordinator: “Can we compare our two intake forms during the call? I have 12 minutes.”
- F2, service owner: “We can inspect the approval steps. We cannot estimate savings until we see completed cases.”
- F3, CRM: No evidence Casey approves a purchase. The call goal is agreement on which redacted form to examine and who can provide it.

**Request.** Write a phone card for this call. Include words I can use for interest, wrong owner, later and no. Leave a blank log I can fill in.

## Decision key

A usable opening is “Casey, you asked to compare two intake forms. Which form should we start with?” It respects a scheduled, 12-minute conversation and does not reintroduce a cold pitch. The opening can be said within 15 seconds. A fit question a buyer can reject is “Is getting the form approved the part worth looking at, or is something else slowing it down?”

| If Casey says | A usable next line |
| --- | --- |
| Interested | “Which redacted example can we review, and who can share it?” Confirm owner and date, then close. |
| Wrong owner | “Who owns the form review, and are you comfortable connecting us?” Close without promising an introduction. |
| Later | “Should we agree on a date, or leave it with you?” Record only the date actually agreed. |
| No | “Understood. Thanks for the time.” End the call. |

The blank log needs outcome, buyer's own words, form chosen, owner, agreed next action and date. Keep unconfirmed fields blank. Do not imply Casey authorized spend or promise a savings figure.

## Edge prompt

Change F1 to a cold outbound call with no prior contact and F3 to “identify the owner.” Rewrite just the opening, the first fork and the wrap-up. Stating that the buyer asked for a call now fails the test. If the person asks never to be called again, log that request and end the call.
