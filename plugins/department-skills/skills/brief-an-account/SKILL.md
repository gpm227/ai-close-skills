# Brief an account before outreach

Write a one-page pre-outreach brief that helps a rep ask a credible question. Use the account identity, the user's offer, target role, as-of date and supplied or authorized research sources. If the actual ask is a conversation script, build facts here and hand the brief to call preparation.

## Investigate the change

- Verify entity, subsidiary and location. Capture facts with a source URL or document/line ID, evidence date and event status. “Plans to open,” “opened” and “hiring” have different implications. Keep the publication date distinct from the event date.
- Prefer primary evidence for the fact at issue. When sources disagree, show what each actually says. A more recent rep conjecture cannot establish that a buyer announced a completed event. If a live source cannot be inspected, work from identifiable supplied extracts and mark what could not be confirmed.
- For each material change, offer two or three hypotheses in total **when supported**. For each, give the supporting fact, possible operational consequence and a concrete observation that would rule it out. With thin evidence, provide fewer hypotheses and a factual discovery question.
- Name the current alternative only if sourced. Distinguish incumbent vendor, internally run process and unknown. Tie the product mechanism to the workflow without asserting the buyer has a problem.

## Deliver the rep brief

Start with the change worth asking about and one neutral opening question. Include the company snapshot, a dated fact ledger, hypotheses with falsifiers and current alternatives. Add one line on what the rep should recheck before contact. Do a final read with the source notes hidden: a rep should be able to tell a buyer-confirmed fact from an inference just by reading the brief. The opening should still work if the buyer rejects your strongest hypothesis.

A company name or website, your offer and the reason you are considering outreach.
