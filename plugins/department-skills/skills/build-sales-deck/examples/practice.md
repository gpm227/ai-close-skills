# An editable decision deck for a pilot sponsor

The fictional buyer has seven minutes. Run the request on the input, then open the [editable HTML decision deck](decision-deck.html). Expand the notes below each slide when presenting from a browser. Print the four audience slides to a 16:9 PDF with browser headers and footers off. The downloadable source is HTML.

## Input

- D1, meeting note from the buyer: “Our director will decide whether to test Friday review at two depots. Can we keep Wednesday's process for the other depots?”
- D2, approved internal sample: Four historical depot-weeks at those two depots. Three contained a reconciliation exception. Sampling was nonrandom. No estimate of cost or network-wide incidence.
- D3, approved offer: Four-week test, two depots, fixed fee of $1,600. Includes setup and weekly check-ins. The remaining sites keep their current process. Sponsor chooses a pilot owner before 20 July; performance criteria are to be agreed.
- D4, reviewer note: The two sample depots may be described in aggregate; no employee or depot identifiers may be shared.
- D5, approved operator docket, header and schedule fields: A blue header names the review schedule. Each line pairs the group with its review day. The printed weekday is monospaced; a narrow rust edge marks an exception to the usual schedule. These conventions can be used in the pilot presentation. No photos of the depots were cleared.

**Request.** Build a short presentation for the director. Include the spoken transition, a likely question on each slide, and what evidence a sponsor needs to sign off.

## Decision key

The title sequence should carry a decision, for example:

1. “Should two depots test a Friday review?” Describe the existing Wednesday process and decision scope (D1).
2. “Three of four sampled depot-weeks contained an exception.” Place “two selected depots; nonrandom historical sample” beside the number (D2). No network-wide loss claim.
3. “The four-week test costs $1,600 and leaves other sites on Wednesday.” Show the status quo alongside the offered pilot; distinguish unagreed success criteria (D3).
4. “Choose a pilot owner by 20 July.” Identify the director's action and what the owner needs to agree before kickoff (D3).

Each slide supplies a source, a distinct spoken note and a plausible question. Verify the D2 caveat remains legible on the actual slide. The deck has one decision and one requested action. If a deck cannot be rendered, deliver the editable slide brief and mark visual QA pending. Do not invent measured savings or expose depot identifiers.

**Check the visuals.** At half size, cover the opening title and ask which group changes day. The D5 docket carries the answer. On slide 2 the tally represents aggregate counts only; a timeline, site map or four named records would imply facts D2 does not contain. At normal presentation size read the sample caveat, price and owner's deadline. Confirm each slide fills exactly one export page and speaker notes stay outside the audience copy. Reuse the decision sequence for another deal, but replace this schedule docket if the new decision has nothing to do with a schedule.

## Edge prompt

The science team withdraws D2 before the meeting. Show how the decision argument and speaker notes change. The pilot and fee remain supported by D1/D3; the number and all claims that rely on it must disappear. Keep any proposed measure of success clearly provisional.
