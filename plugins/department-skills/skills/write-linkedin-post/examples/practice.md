# Practice · Turn a founder's desk note into a post

## Run this request

“Write a 120–150 word LinkedIn post for print shop owners. My point is that I need to compare the same days of each month before changing the schedule.” Fictional input:

- D1, author's desk note cleared for public use: “On Friday I caught myself comparing twelve days of reprints to all of April. I pulled April 1–12. Both windows had nine reprints. I left Monday’s shift alone. The report did not tell me why any job came back.”
- V1, authored style samples: “I checked the sheet again.” / “Nine orders. I can work with that.” Tone is terse, first-person and concrete.
- C1, private client anecdote: A customer asked for a refund. The author did not witness it. Exclude it.

## The founder’s post

On Friday I nearly changed Monday’s shift because I read the reprint report wrong.

I had twelve days of reprints on one side and the whole of April on the other. I went back and pulled April 1–12. Nine reprints then. Nine in the current twelve days. I left Monday’s shift alone.

That comparison answered the question I had about the count. It couldn’t tell me why any of those jobs came back, or whether nine was a good number. I’d need to look at the jobs themselves for that.

Next time I open the report, the first thing I want to see is the date range on each side. I can decide what to do with the number once I know which days I’m looking at.

**Alternate first line.** Nine reprints nearly changed Monday’s shift.

**Author notes.** The event, counts, dates and shift decision come from D1. The final paragraph is a proposed personal takeaway for author review. V1 informed the short paired count sentences. There is no direct quote, customer name, client story or claim about how all print shops operate.
