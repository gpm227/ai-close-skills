# Rivet Bench · stage-flat repair intake

Rivet Bench is a fictional scenery repair shop. A stage manager has a broken scenic flat. Build an editable brand guide and a one-page repair intake proof they can use to photograph the damage and request a plan.

**Deck, page 2 (private).** “Last season 18 pieces were back in two days.” That was one tour. “Never promise a completion date before inspection.” Keep the figure out of public copy.

**Website capture, Repairs (public practice evidence).** “Show us the break.” The shop accepts a photo, inspects the joint and sends a repair plan before the stage manager approves the work. The intake form accepts repair photos at https://example.org/repair. No company-wide font specification or original logo was supplied. The capture shows the intake destination. Its upload capacity and live submission have not been tested. Treat the two views as preparation guidance.

**Workshop source (cleared for this practice job).** Inspect the [editable repair intake plate](rivet-intake-plate.svg) and its [PNG export](rivet-intake-plate.png). It shows the back of one scenic flat, the marked joint in its actual location and the same opening enlarged. The two views explain what should be visible in the submitted photos. Its annotations, color roles and type apply to this drawing; their use elsewhere requires a separate decision.

**Design standard RB-2, November 23, 2026.** An earlier generic serif sheet on a warm paper field was rejected. The new proof must make the photo directions visible in the first scan and put the actual action within reach. Begin with the marked flat or its photographic equivalent. The full-frame and joint views must show the same location. Use the bright surface, assertive hierarchy and evident action of the owner's approved reference as a composition test. The original reference's logo, photograph, accent color and headline belong to that business. Use the shop's own cleared drawing here. If a bicycle repair shop can use the graphic unchanged, redraw it.

Use workshop language, one action and three distinct steps. Keep total visible instructional copy, including words inside the figure, within 55 words. A second writer should be able to draft a follow-up without inventing turnaround. Preserve the first and revised comps for separate reviews. The owner decides whether a template is ready.
